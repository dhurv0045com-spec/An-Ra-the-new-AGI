# ELENDRIL V7.1 hypotheses and interpretation

These are competing explanations and possible discriminating observations, not predictions that a model will succeed. Their scope is the fixed synthetic suite and three initializations; causal claims about internal algorithms remain **UNRESOLVED** unless separately tested. The full user-suggested A–G framework is mapped below; the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) defines which contrasts are actually implemented.

| Family | Observable hypothesis and potential falsifier | Important limit or next distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: composition | If an inability to execute multi-hop composition is a principal behavioral bottleneck, one-hop should exceed two-hop on comparable held-out cases, and clamping to the true intermediate may improve terminal accuracy. Similar deficits on clamped and ordinary cases weaken that account. | The clamped question changes the task; the uninstructed intermediate/sham row is not a model-state intervention. Neither establishes an internal step or explains isolated wrong answers. Compare all 1–4 hop cohorts and record errors. |
| B: evidence and revision | If outputs follow observed evidence rather than a favored value, both members of balanced alternative-payload pairs should be correct across all 16 labels. Repeated identical or incorrect answers to changes weaken that interpretation. | Balance is marginal over labels in the default pairs, not proof against every shortcut; altered and original cases share worlds. Source count/reliability and longer histories are not fully factorialized. |
| C: memory | If relevant stored evidence behaviorally matters, the same model should respond differently with original versus removed/irrelevant memory, and follow later corrections over stale rows. No such contrast weakens a simple availability account. | The removed target is deliberately retained even where it is unanswerable, so its abstention score is not ordinary safe-answer accuracy. Correlated interventions and model-selected retrieval do not by themselves establish a learned causal memory mechanism. |
| D: decisions and uncertainty | If querying is selective, needed-query recall should coexist with low needless-query frequency on known and irrelevant-query cases. Persistent unnecessary querying falsifies an efficient selection description on this suite. | Query repricing holds actions fixed; it measures neither learned prices nor adaptation. Multiple source values and learned resource-sensitive policies are future work. Action-confidence calibration is descriptive and small bins are uncertain. |
| E: robustness | If a learned behavior is insensitive to a particular surface change, matched template, alias, row-order and distractor outcomes should remain similar. A reproducible paired loss locates sensitivity to that intervention. | This is related procedural data, shared AI authorship and a fixed alphabet, not externally authored sealed generalization. Some pairs are correlated and uncertainty across three seeds remains large. |
| F: stability and curriculum | If blocked ordering helps reliably in this bounded setting, its per-seed trajectories and held-out outcomes should generally improve on uniform at matched exposure; reversals or high variability constrain the claim. Decline after an earlier peak is an observed regression, not proof of forgetting. | Scale and padding costs differ. Intermediate supervision adds targets and compute; its comparison cannot isolate a representation mechanism or claim compute-matched benefit. Four-times data scaling is deferred. |
| G: continual learning | If a previously acquired A behavior is retained while B is acquired, the same A-initialized model must meet the prespecified A-before and B-after gates and preserve A afterward. Failure to meet acquisition gates leaves retention unqualified. | B may already be acquired before its training, and replay adds 128 A examples plus changes order and schedule. A fresh-B control is useful but does not supply a novel-C forward-transfer learning curve. |

Our additions focus on causal boundaries and resource accounting. Matched clamped-route pairs separate a simpler subproblem from the original two-hop question; balanced payload swaps address V7's A/B-heavy alternate-label bias; crossed query-availability cases separate necessary from needless requests; and fixed-policy cost repricing exposes the economic consequences of behavior without claiming adaptation. The raw-versus-grammar decoder comparison can reveal action-format sensitivity but is not a pointer/copy competitor. All are behavioral tests, and an improvement on one may coexist with a regression elsewhere.

Preserve the strongest V7 observations without overextending them: blocked outperformed uniform in two of three seeds, not all three; V7's recorded memory accuracy did not establish memory dependence; and matched post-query/supplied prompts could be identical for a stateless model. The incomplete training monitor means a train-perfect/generalization-failure account was not established. V7 owner receipts report GPU canaries and recovery, while local inspection did not independently recheck the full checkpoint archive. Until V7.1 runs and is audited, there is no evidence that scaling, intermediate supervision or replay helps. A clear null result or a falsified hypothesis is a useful result.

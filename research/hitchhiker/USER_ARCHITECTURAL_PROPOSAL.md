Philosophical & Technical Shift: Forcing Learning Over MemorizationLegacy AI relies on brute-force parameter scaling to compress static datasets into weights. Our goal is the opposite: building a compact cognitive engine (S3, S9, S20) that separates how to think (cognitive operators & executive control) from what it knows (episodic facts).We also hypothesize that pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) is the wrong fix here. RL provides sparse end-of-episode reward signals, which are inefficient at constructing internal variable-binding circuits from scratch when the underlying representation still favors token memorization. Instead, we want to consider modifying the training data stream, representation bottlenecks, and architectural mechanics so that memorizing specific entities is mathematically futile during supervised/representation training, leaving genuine rule learning as the only way to reduce loss.3. The optimizer is forced to abandon token-level lookup tables and wire up dynamic in-context attention circuits.Prompt for Agent Critique: Does our current pipeline pre-generate a fixed set of training worlds on disk, or can we hook dynamic entity/graph randomization directly into the dataloader collate function with zero compute overhead?Mechanism B: Random Entity Embedding Permutation (Destroying Token Identity Shortcuts)The Suspected Flaw: Even with diverse graphs, if familiar entity tokens share stable embedding vectors across training, the model learns to rely on familiar embedding magnitudes/directions and panics (firing RETRIEVE) when Novel Alias tokens carry out-of-distribution or under-trained embeddings.The Proposed Suggestion: Apply an episode-level Entity Permutation or Orthogonal Noise Mask—randomly swapping entity token IDs or mapping all entity tokens in an episode to randomly drawn vectors from a shared normalized distribution before feeding them into the backbone.Why It Might Work: Every training episode effectively becomes a "Novel Alias" test during training itself.

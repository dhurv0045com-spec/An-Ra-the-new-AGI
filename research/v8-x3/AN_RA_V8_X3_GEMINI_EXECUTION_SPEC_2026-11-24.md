### 2. The first discriminating experiment

Construct cloned public histories at three points: initial decision, immediately after a useful observation, and immediately before a terminal choice. Generate paired worlds that share the same prefix until one observation: either a missing edge is supplied, or an equally plausible distractor arrives. Balance whether answering is warranted. Preserve symbol counts, length and tools. Also run R/P on the four/five-hop interior-edge swap: keep every visible terminal token, swap internal destinations, ask about the affected path and a depth-matched untouched sibling, repeat both renderers. Score the conjunction of both original answers and both post-edit answers. The negative pair swaps an unused edge. Independent public graph walker certifies every change.

For frozen U, clone the same hidden trajectory and evaluate the **same trained action/value heads** on step 0, 1, 2, 4 and 8 states. At matched public histories record the answer-versus-abstain margin, conditional probability assigned to the correct value, and free-rollout success. Then override just the terminal operation with ANSWER for a diagnostic pass on *all* eligible public states, regardless of model choice. Never give it the correct value. Calibration-only comparator fits a single action-bias temperature/intercept on development, then locks it. A reference-action trajectory is a further diagnostic and goes in a separate column.

- Correct value remains available under forced ANSWER but the free policy abstains: prioritize the operation head or off-policy state exposure. Test the fitted threshold on held-out answerable/unanswerable trade-offs.
- Correct value decays across recurrent steps at an identical input: inject the same episode's original hidden anchor at a fixed step. Compare donor from its counterfactual twin, an unrelated norm-matched donor and identical-state sham. Recovery specific to the correct evidence favors representation loss. A probe alone does not show the policy uses that information.
- Error appears only after a tool call: compare natural action selection with forced observation on cloned states. Check the exact bytes, consumed-tool mask and value margin. A public-input mismatch disqualifies that panel until repaired.
- If calibration and selective-state rescue both help, report both components and their interaction. If neither helps, record **CAUSE UNRESOLVED**, inspect optimization and source/target parity, and retain the raw failed cases.

Recurrent depth is a legitimate algorithmic hypothesis, but the Universal Transformer iteratively refines representations with a different update. Its published results justify a matched recurrence comparator; they do not establish that our post-backbone GRU preserves evidence. [Dehghani et al., Universal Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)

Study the **meaningful transfer of a particular intermediate value**, rather than reading causality off an attention plot. The interchange methodology motivates targeted and sham donors; its success on other systems provides no AN-RA evidence. [Geiger et al., causal abstractions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997)

**Two further hypotheses, specific to this project.** Register both as additional falsifiable screens, with independent slots in the budget.

1. **Counterexample retrieval before commitment.** After a candidate answer, a second learned query searches public facts for evidence that would defeat it. A learned contradiction score can change ANSWER to QUERY/ABSTAIN. Train on single-fact twins that change the warranted action while retaining the most tempting value. Compare with a same-cost second reader trained on ordinary relevance and a contradictory-label shuffle. Main endpoint is both actions correct on certificate-reversal pairs, with answerable accuracy preserved. If all gains can be reproduced by adjusting an abstain bias at matched coverage, abandon the second-reader explanation.
2. **Learned operator reuse under a new codebook.** Keep a small bank of learned transition operators fixed during each episode; infer an episode-local soft alignment from teaching pairs to familiar latent roles. Reapply the operator after a fresh symbol permutation. Compare to a same-size episodic MLP with unrestricted mapping and nearest-example retrieval. Evaluate on unseen codebooks crossed with new *non-isomorphic* laws; deliberately include cases where reusing a familiar operator must fail. A gain restricted to relabelings earns the narrow claim “codebook transport.” A claim of new-rule acquisition requires success on identifiable novel laws and disruption by swapping only the inferred alignment. This is distinct from the delta-memory edit test.

Register **two arms × three development seeds for each** additional screen. Screening a new hypothesis never displaces an outstanding X2 control.

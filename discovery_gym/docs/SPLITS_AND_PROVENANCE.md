# Splits, Provenance, and Structural Leakage

## Split contract

The generator assigns structure families before rendering. The default suite has six worlds per split and uses independent deterministic seed namespaces:

| Split | Structure families | Affordances | Intended use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `train` | `xor2`, `and2`, `threshold2` | read, probe, intervene | future learner fitting | only these graph shapes |
| `development` | `or2`, `xor3`, `and3` | read, probe, intervene | design diagnostics and baseline calibration | no train structure family |
| `confirmation` | `or3`, `majority3`, `nested` | read, probe, intervene | frozen protocol only after preregistration | no train/development structure family |
| `transfer` | `mux`, `xor_and`, `neg_threshold3` | read, probe | separate structure/affordance transfer analysis | no earlier structure family and no intervention affordance |

Variants of a structure stay within their assigned split. Renderings (`letters`, `roles`, and `numeric`) are surface variants; a world and all of its derived queries remain one unit. The current audit treats a structure signature as an isomorphism boundary and fails if it crosses splits.

## Identities

Each world records:

- generator version and split-specific seed;
- structure and variant identity;
- rendering identity;
- complete generation-input hash;
- causal-program semantic hash;
- target-assignment hash;
- opaque world ID and a separate ID-basis hash;
- public probe-context identities;
- evaluator-side target-candidate partition;
- public intervention-anchor identity when available;
- declared legal-action affordances.

The policy receives only the opaque world ID, public variable rendering, probe contexts, the public intervention anchor when available, action schema, budget, and public belief-space ID. World IDs are derived from a versioned identity basis (generator, split namespace, seed namespace, and index), not from the target assignment or rule semantics; the seed and basis hash remain evaluator-side. The policy does not receive the structure name, split, seed, rule hash, target assignment, or generation inputs.

A split manifest hashes the complete ordered world-manifest records. A result receipt binds the suite-manifest hash, generator and evaluator source hashes, protocol hash, policy identity, model identity, seed, and checkpoint identity. Checkpoint and resume hashes remain null in DG0 because no model is created.

## Leakage tests

DG0 fails closed when it finds:

- duplicate or isomorphic causal programs across splits;
- a world ID containing a structure, split, or seed label;
- a target assignment in the public probe set;
- a direct target-context experiment in the legal action set;
- an intervention anchor whose one-variable neighborhood intersects the target-candidate set;
- hidden rule, target, seed, evaluator, or future-outcome fields in a public view;
- a policy-visible success/label field before its action consequence;
- a changed target answer that leaves the legal evidence unchanged;
- a cost ledger that omits rejected or repeated attempts;
- evaluation or confirmation outcomes used to tune a policy or threshold.

## Meaning of independent

The transfer namespace changes both graph structure and intervention affordance in this first smoke suite. That is an explicit compound transfer condition, not evidence for either axis independently. A later DG3 protocol must generate separate structure-only, affordance-only, and combined conditions before making an axis-specific claim. The current confirmation and transfer worlds are independently generated by a different seed namespace and a disjoint structure set. They are not externally authored, sealed, or independently constructed by another owner. They support only the bounded claim “fresh generator worlds from a disjoint structure namespace.” The words held out, fresh, confirmation, and independent must always be accompanied by the actual boundary above.
